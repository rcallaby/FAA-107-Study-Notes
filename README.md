# FAA-107-Study-Notes

Study notes for the FAA **Remote Pilot Certificate** with a **Small UAS rating** (Part 107).

The Unmanned Aircraft General – Small (`UAG`) knowledge test is built on manned-aviation rules because small unmanned aircraft share the National Airspace System with commercial and general aviation traffic. These notes exist so you can pass on the first try **and** understand the safety framework behind the questions.

This repository is a study aid. The controlling sources are always:

- [14 CFR Part 107](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-107)
- [Remote Pilot ACS (FAA-S-ACS-10B)](https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/acs/uas_acs.pdf)
- [Become a Certificated Remote Pilot](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot)

## Exam facts

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Test name | Unmanned Aircraft General – Small (`UAG`) |
| Questions | 60 scored multiple-choice (plus unscored validation items) |
| Time | 2 hours |
| Passing score | **70%** (42 / 60) |
| Minimum age to test | 14 |
| Minimum age for the certificate | **16** |
| Typical PSI fee | about **$175** per attempt |
| Certificate fee | **$0** via IACRA |
| Retake wait | **14 calendar days** |
| Recency after you pass | free online recurrent training every **24 calendar months** (`ALC-677`, or `ALC-515` if you also hold a current Part 61 flight review) |
| What you get at the center | Airman Knowledge Testing Supplement (`FAA-CT-8080-2H`) with charts, legends, and weather figures |

Approximate ACS weighting:

| Area | Typical share |
|---|---|
| I. Regulations | 15–25% |
| II. Airspace and operating requirements | 15–25% |
| III. Weather | 11–16% |
| IV. Loading and performance | 7–11% |
| V. Operations (radio, airports, emergencies, ADM, physiology, maintenance, night) | 35–45% |

Operations is the largest bucket. Do not skip charts, airports, night operations, CRM, or emergencies.

**Ready-to-schedule bar:** two timed practice tests at **85%+**, plus the number table below recited from memory.

## How to use these notes

1. Read [Exam cram](#exam-cram) until the numbers are automatic.
2. Work through the [full outline](#full-study-outline) and check every bullet against current Part 107 / the ACS.
3. Drill sectional figures from `FAA-CT-8080-2H` every day.
4. Keep an error log by ACS code (`UA.I.B.K18`, `UA.II.A.K1c`, and so on).
5. Reconcile any older study-guide language (especially night operations, operations over people, and Remote ID) with the **current** eCFR text.

## First-try study plan

**Week 1 — rules and airspace**

- Read 14 CFR Part 107 end to end.
- Read ACS Areas I and II.
- Take free `ALC-451` on [FAASafety.gov](https://www.faasafety.gov).
- Identify airspace on 10 testing-supplement excerpts per day.

**Week 2 — weather, loading, charts**

- PHAK weather and airport chapters.
- METAR / TAF decoding until it is mechanical.
- Density altitude and CG explained out loud with no notes.
- Daily chart questions until floor / ceiling misses stop.

**Week 3 — operations and mixed exams**

- ADM, CRM, night physiology, emergencies, radio, airport markings.
- Two timed 60-question exams.
- Restudy only missed ACS codes.

## Exam cram

### Memorize these numbers first

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Max groundspeed | **87 kt / 100 mph** |
| Max altitude | **400 ft AGL** |
| Structure exception | Within **400 ft** of a structure, not more than **400 ft above** the top |
| Min visibility (from the control station) | **3 SM** |
| Cloud clearance | **500 ft below**, **2,000 ft horizontally** |
| Anti-collision light (night / twilight) | Visible **3 SM** |
| Accident report deadline | **10 calendar days** |
| Property-damage report trigger | **> $500** repair **or** fair market value (not the drone itself) |
| Injury report trigger | **Serious injury** or **loss of consciousness** |
| sUAS weight limit | **< 55 lb** including everything attached |
| Category 1 over people | **≤ 0.55 lb** |
| Registration | **$5 / 3 years**, per aircraft under Part 107 |
| Knowledge recency | Recurrent training every **24 calendar months** |
| Alcohol memory item | **8 hours**, **0.04% BAC**, no impairing drugs |
| Dark adaptation | about **30 minutes** |

Structure trick: you may fly *higher than 400 AGL* only inside a 400-ft-radius cylinder around a structure, and never more than 400 ft above that structure.

### Who can fly / papers

- Need a Remote Pilot Certificate with an sUAS rating, or fly under a remote PIC who can take over immediately.
- English + fit to fly. No FAA medical, but you cannot fly with a known unsafe condition.
- Certificate and ID readily available when flying.
- Falsifying certificates or records can cost you the certificate.
- **Part 107 registration is required even under 0.55 lb.** The recreational under-0.55 exemption does **not** apply.
- Part 48 (FAADroneZone) = under 55 lb. Part 47 = 55 lb+ / special cases.
- Mark the registration on the outside. Carry proof.
- Remote PIC owns condition for safe flight, weather, airspace, people on the ground, crew briefing, and the land-now decision.

### Operating rules in one page

- **VLOS:** unaided vision (corrective lenses OK). Binoculars / FPV do **not** satisfy VLOS. Someone must know position, attitude, altitude, and direction of flight.
- FPV requires a visual observer who keeps unaided VLOS.
- PIC, person at the controls, and VO must be able to communicate.
- **One sUAS at a time** for PIC, manipulator, or VO.
- Yield to **all** manned aircraft. Do not create a collision hazard.
- No careless or reckless operation.
- No hazardous materials.
- No dropping objects that hazard people or property.
- No flying from a moving aircraft.
- Moving land / water vehicle: only over a **sparsely populated** area, and **not** carrying property for hire — unless waived.
- Do not interfere with airport operations or traffic patterns.
- Prohibited / restricted areas need the using agency’s permission.
- TFRs and NOTAMs are mandatory. Check every flight.
- External load is OK if secure and does not hurt controllability.
- In-state property transport is OK if total weight stays **< 55 lb** and other rules are met.
- Emergency: deviate as needed to meet the emergency; report if the FAA asks.
- No transponder and no ADS-B Out unless authorized.
- Make the aircraft available to the FAA for inspection.

**Night / civil twilight**

- Legal if the remote PIC completed an initial test or recurrent training **after April 6, 2021** that includes night operations, **and** the aircraft has anti-collision lighting visible **3 SM**.
- Night does **not** waive VLOS, weather minima, airspace authorization, or over-people rules.
- If the light fails, land.

### Over people / moving vehicles

Default: do **not** fly over people unless they are:

1. directly participating in the operation, or
2. under a covered structure / inside a **stationary** vehicle that can protect them, or
3. the operation meets Category 1–4.

“Over” means over any part of a person, even briefly.

| Category | Aircraft test | Operational catch |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ≤ **0.55 lb**, no skin-lacerating exposed rotors | Sustained flight over open-air assemblies needs Remote ID compliance |
| **2** | Accepted Declaration of Compliance; impact ≤ **11 ft-lb**; no lacerating rotors | Labeling, remote-pilot operating instructions, maintenance |
| **3** | Same injury idea as Category 2 | Usually a closed / restricted site with notice, or **no sustained flight** over people; not for open-air assemblies |
| **4** | Part 21 airworthiness certificate + Flight Manual allows it | Follow the certificated limitations |

Moving vehicles must fit a valid category. Categories 1–3: closed / restricted site with notice, **or** no **sustained** flight over moving vehicles.

Modifying the aircraft can void category eligibility.

### Remote ID (Part 89)

- Part 107 flights need Standard Remote ID or a broadcast module.
- Standard RID broadcasts required message elements from takeoff to shutdown.
- Broadcast-module operations typically remain within VLOS of the person at the controls.
- Product labeling / RID serial number matters.
- ADS-B Out is **not** Remote ID.
- Confirm RID is working before takeoff.
- Do not assume a FRIA lets commercial Part 107 work skip RID.

### Waiver vs authorization

| Need | Tool |
|---|---|
| Enter Class **B, C, D, or E to the surface** | **Authorization** (LAANC or FAADroneZone) |
| Break a Part 107 operating rule | **Waiver** |

LAANC approval does **not** let you bust VLOS, cloud clearance, or over-people rules.

Commonly waivable sections include moving-vehicle rules, the night-light provision, VLOS, VO rules, multiple sUAS, right-of-way, over people, certain airspace, §107.51 limits, and over moving vehicles. A VLOS waiver will **not** be issued to carry someone else’s property for compensation or hire.

### Airspace cram

Authorization is required in **B, C, D, and Class E surface areas**.  
Class G needs no class-based ATC authorization. Part 107 weather minima still apply.

| Class | Chart cue | Typical shape | sUAS need |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | Solid **blue** | Shelves; `100/30` means 10,000 ceiling / 3,000 floor **MSL** | Authorization |
| C | Solid **magenta** | Core + shelf (often SFC–4,000 AAE) | Authorization |
| D | Dashed **blue** | Often SFC–2,500 AAE | Authorization |
| E surface | Dashed **magenta** | Around some airports | Authorization |
| E 700 | **Magenta** vignette | Floor 700 AGL | No class auth *below* that floor |
| E 1,200 | **Blue** vignette | Floor 1,200 AGL | No class auth *below* that floor |
| G | Remainder under those floors | Uncontrolled | No class auth |

Special-use memory:

- **P-** prohibited: stay out
- **R-** restricted: controlling agency when hot
- **W-** warning: offshore military
- **MOA / Alert:** caution
- **CFA:** not charted
- **NSA:** requested avoidance
- **TFR:** NOTAM, still binding
- **MTR IR/VR:** low, fast military traffic

Mode C veil (~30 NM around Class B) is a manned transponder concept. You still need §107.41 authorization to enter B / C / D / E-surface.

When the tower closes, Class D often reverts to E or G. Read the chart and Chart Supplement.

### Sectional symbols that get asked

- Blue airport = tower exists (check hours). Magenta airport = no tower.
- Airport number pair: field elevation / longest runway (runway in hundreds of feet).
- Star = rotating beacon.
- Obstacle `1504 (499)` = **1,504 MSL**, **499 AGL**.
- MEF = highest terrain / obstacle in that quadrangle plus a buffer. Not a 400-ft clearance card.
- Magenta flag = VFR checkpoint.
- Use the scale bar. Do not guess distance.
- Isogonic line = magnetic variation. Runway numbers are magnetic.
- Always open the figure. Printed floors beat “typical” memorized dimensions.

### Weather cram

Go / no-go: **3 SM** visibility from the control station, plus **500 / 2,000** cloud clearance.

- Ceiling = lowest **BKN or OVC**
- Hot + high + humid + low pressure = high density altitude = less lift and less endurance
- Unstable air: turbulence, showers, towering clouds, good visibility between showers
- Stable air: smooth, layered clouds, poor visibility, fog / haze
- Cold front: narrow, gusty, cumulonimbus, then clearing / cooler
- Warm front: stratiform, steady precip, poor visibility
- METAR wind is **from** that direction, in degrees **true**
- Thunderstorm stages: cumulus → mature → dissipating. Microbursts live in the mature stage.

Example METAR:

```text
KABC 181856Z 18012G20KT 3SM -RA BR BKN008 OVC015 12/11 A2984
```

- 18th day, 1856Z
- Wind 180 at 12, gust 20
- 3 SM, light rain, mist
- Broken 800, overcast 1,500 → ceiling **800 AGL**
- Temp 12 / dew point 11 (tight spread = fog risk)
- Altimeter 29.84

TAF: `FM` = from, `TEMPO` = temporary, `BECMG` = becoming, `PROB30` = 30% chance.

### Loading / batteries / link

- Weight includes battery, payload, and mounts.
- CG offset = instability, extra motor load, shorter flight.
- A shifting or falling payload is both a performance problem and a dropping-object problem.
- LiPo swelling, odor, or dents = do not fly. Charge in a safe container. Store at storage voltage. Cold packs sag. Crashed packs are a delayed fire risk.
- Lost link / RTH / GPS loss: the PIC still owns the outcome.
- Automation does not transfer responsibility.
- Anyone may maintain a typical Part 107 sUAS. The remote PIC still decides if it is safe.

### Airports and radio

- Monitor CTAF or tower near airports even if you never transmit.
- Standard pattern is left traffic unless charted otherwise.
- Typical airplane pattern altitude is about 1,000 AGL. A 400-ft drone can still sit on final or departure.
- CTAF = nontowered advisory. UNICOM = FBO / advisory. ATIS = recorded airport info.
- Civil beacon: white-green. Military: extra white. Heliport: white-green-yellow.
- Chart Supplement = tower hours, frequencies, remarks.

### ADM / human factors

**IMSAFE:** Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion / Eating.

Hazardous attitudes and antidotes:

- Anti-authority → Follow the rules
- Impulsivity → Not so fast. Think first
- Invulnerability → It could happen to me
- Macho → Taking chances is foolish
- Resignation → I can make a difference

**DECIDE:** Detect, Estimate, Choose, Identify, Do, Evaluate  
**PAVE:** Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures

Night vision: off-center viewing, empty-field myopia, autokinesis, protect dark adaptation. If the label says drowsiness, do not fly.

### Reporting

Report to the FAA within **10 calendar days** if the operation involves:

- serious injury **or** any loss of consciousness, or
- damage to property other than the small UA unless repair cost or FMV is **$500 or less**

A destroyed drone with no injury and no other property damage does **not** by itself trigger §107.9.

## Core skills

These are the skills the test uses to ask every other question.

**Spatial orientation**

- Turn a 2-D sectional into 3-D floors, ceilings, shelves, and rings.
- Visualize the aircraft relative to you, a runway, a Class D surface area, and a nearby Class B shelf.
- Translate the 400-ft structure exception into a mental cylinder.

**Meteorology**

- Decode METAR / TAF groups without guessing.
- Connect density altitude to lift, hover margin, and battery endurance.
- Treat fronts, wind shear, mechanical turbulence, and thunderstorms as go / no-go items.

**Risk management**

- Separate a hazard (condition) from risk (probability × severity).
- Treat client pressure, fatigue, and “just get the shot” as accident precursors.

**Regulatory navigation**

- Read definitions first, then operating rules, then exceptions.
- Know the difference between a **waiver** and an **airspace authorization**.

## Full study outline

### 1. Regulations

ACS Area I. Know who the rules apply to and why they exist.

**Applicability and definitions**

- Civil small unmanned aircraft operations in the United States.
- Small unmanned aircraft: less than 55 lb at takeoff, including everything on board or attached.
- Small UAS: aircraft plus associated elements required for safe operation.
- Part 107 does not apply to air carrier operations, 49 U.S.C. 44809 recreational operations, certain 333 / 44807 exemption operations, or Part 91 operations of an sUAS that has an airworthiness certificate.
- Visual observer: person designated by the remote PIC to help see and avoid traffic and objects.
- Remote PIC: final authority over the operation.
- Another person may manipulate the controls only if the remote PIC can immediately take over.

**Certification**

- Age 16, English, fit to fly, pass `UAG` **or** complete `ALC-451` if you hold a qualifying current Part 61 certificate.
- Aeronautical knowledge recency: required online recurrent training within the previous 24 calendar months.
- Alcohol / drug offenses and refusing a test can bar or revoke certification.
- Falsification of a certificate, authorization, record, or report is its own violation.

**Registration**

- Part 107 operations require registration of the aircraft, including aircraft under 0.55 lb.
- Part 48 online registration at FAADroneZone for most sUAS under 55 lb: about $5 per aircraft, valid 3 years, unique number marked on the exterior.
- Part 47 paper / N-number path for 55 lb or more and certain ownership situations.
- Recreational registration is per owner. Part 107 registration is per aircraft.

**Remote PIC duties (§107.19, §107.49)**

- Ensure the sUAS is in a condition for safe operation.
- Assess weather, airspace, flight restrictions, people and property, and ground hazards.
- Inspect aircraft, control station, links, power, and payload security.
- Brief everyone directly participating on conditions, roles, hazards, and emergencies.
- Land if the operation becomes unsafe.
- §107.17: do not fly with a known physical or mental condition that would interfere with safety.

**Hard limits (§107.51)**

- 87 kt / 100 mph groundspeed.
- 400 ft AGL, unless within a 400-ft radius of a structure and not more than 400 ft above that structure’s immediate uppermost limit.
- 3 SM visibility from the control station.
- 500 ft below clouds and 2,000 ft horizontally.

**Other operating rules to be able to quote**

- VLOS, VO, one-aircraft-at-a-time, right-of-way, airports, prohibited / restricted areas, NOTAM restrictions, no HAZMAT, no careless / reckless operation, no hazardous dropped objects, moving-vehicle limits, alcohol / drugs, transponder / ADS-B Out prohibition, emergency deviation.

**Operations over people**

- Categories 1–4, kinetic-energy idea (11 ft-lb for Categories 2 and 3), exposed rotating parts, Declaration of Compliance, Means of Compliance, labeling, maintenance, modifications, closed / restricted-access sites, sustained flight, moving vehicles, open-air assemblies.

**Remote ID**

- Standard RID vs broadcast module vs FRIA concept.
- Required message-element idea: unique ID, UA location / altitude / velocity, control station or takeoff point, time mark, emergency status.
- Preflight confirmation that RID is functioning.

**Reports, inspections, waivers**

- §107.9 10-day report.
- Produce aircraft and records on request.
- Waiver list vs LAANC / DroneZone authorization.

### 2. Airspace

ACS Area II.

- ATC manages traffic in controlled airspace. You still see and avoid.
- Class A exists (18,000 ft MSL and above). Part 107 almost never goes there, but do not confuse it with other classes on a chart.
- Controlled airspace classes B, C, D, and E: vertical / lateral dimensions, floor / ceiling language, communication vs authorization.
- Class G: surface up to the overlying controlled airspace, often 700 or 1,200 ft AGL.
- Special-use airspace: prohibited, restricted, warning, MOA, alert, CFA.
- Other airspace: TFR, MTR, NSA, parachute jump areas, TRSA, wildlife / park notes.
- NOTAM system and how to obtain current NOTAMs.
- LAANC grid authorizations and FAADroneZone when automated approval is not available.
- Night equipment and unlighted ground hazards as airspace-adjacent operating requirements.

### 3. Charts and navigation

The testing supplement is part of the exam.

- Latitude, longitude, meridians, parallels, scale bar.
- Airport markers: towered vs nontowered, military, private, seaplane, heliport.
- Obstruction heights, lighting, group obstacles, power lines, VFR checkpoints.
- Airspace print language: solid blue / magenta, dashed blue / magenta, vignettes.
- MEF.
- Isogonic lines and magnetic vs true.
- Chart Supplement as the source for hours, frequencies, and remarks.

### 4. Weather

ACS Area III.

**Sources**

- Internet briefings, Flight Service / 1800wxbrief, METAR, TAF, AIRMET, SIGMET, Convective SIGMET, PIREPs, GFA, surface and prognostic charts, ASOS / AWOS.

**Effects on performance**

- Density altitude.
- Wind, gradient, mechanical turbulence, thermals, wind shear, microbursts.
- Air masses and fronts.
- Ceiling and visibility.
- Thunderstorms, lightning, hail, icing, tornadoes, fog.

**Atmosphere basics**

- Highs vs lows.
- Standard atmosphere: 15°C / 29.92 inHg at sea level.
- Stable vs unstable air.
- Cloud types worth knowing: stratus, nimbostratus, cumulus, towering cumulus, cumulonimbus.

### 5. Loading, performance, and systems

ACS Area IV plus the systems items that show up under Operations.

- Effects of loading change.
- Balance, stability, and center of gravity.
- Use manufacturer performance data when it exists.
- Lithium battery inspection, charging, storage, voltage sag, thermal runaway, transport, post-crash fire risk.
- RF spectrum, 2.4 GHz vs 5.8 GHz, interference, multipath, antenna orientation.
- Lost link, flyaway, GPS loss, RTH failure modes.
- Firmware, compass / IMU, control-station failure.
- Basic maintenance, who may perform it, recordkeeping, night-specific preflight (lights).

### 6. Operations, ADM, and human factors

ACS Area V. Largest exam slice.

**Radio**

- Towered vs nontowered.
- CTAF, UNICOM, ATIS, ground, tower, approach.
- Phonetic alphabet.
- Phraseology for altitude, direction, speed, and time.
- Self-announce position and intentions.
- Monitor to build traffic picture even when you are not talking.

**Airports**

- Traffic patterns and typical altitudes.
- Runway markings and signs.
- Airport and seaplane lighting.
- Pilot-controlled lighting.
- SIDA.
- Wildlife hazards and strike reporting.
- Chart Supplement / airport data sources.

**Emergencies**

- Lost link, flyaway, battery failure, GPS loss, magnetic interference.
- Aircraft drifting toward people or an active runway.
- Lithium fire.
- Night lighting failure.
- Who to call: local ATC near an airport, 911 for injuries, FAA if §107.9 is met.

**ADM and CRM**

- IMSAFE.
- Five hazardous attitudes.
- DECIDE, PAVE, 3P / CARE-style models.
- Situational awareness and task management.
- Crew briefing, sector scan, closed-loop communication.
- External pressures from clients.

**Physiology**

- Fatigue, dehydration, heat, stress, hyperventilation.
- Drugs, alcohol, prescription and OTC medications.
- Vision: photopic / mesopic / scotopic, rods vs cones, off-center night scan.
- Night illusions: empty-field myopia, autokinesis, false horizon.
- Fitness for flight as a remote PIC duty.

## Certification process

**First-time remote pilots**

1. Create an IACRA account and get an FTN **before** scheduling.
2. Book `UAG` at [faa.psiexams.com](https://faa.psiexams.com).
3. Pass the knowledge test.
4. File FAA Form 8710-13 in IACRA. The 17-digit exam ID can take up to 48 hours to appear.
5. Complete TSA security vetting.
6. Print the temporary certificate from IACRA when instructed.
7. Permanent plastic card arrives by mail.
8. Keep the certificate available whenever you operate.
9. Complete recurrent online training every 24 calendar months.

**Existing Part 61 pilots** with a flight review in the previous 24 months take `ALC-451` instead of `UAG`, then validate identity with an FSDO, DPE, ACR, or CFI and file 8710-13.

Required flying kit after certification: remote pilot certificate, photo ID, aircraft registration, a RID-compliant aircraft, and any needed airspace authorization.

## Why the FAA built the test this way

Part 107 is not a camera-operator quiz. It is an entry ticket into the same NAS used by airliners, air ambulances, crop-dusters, and student pilots in the pattern.

- **Shared language.** Sectional symbols, METAR groups, CTAF calls, and airspace names let a remote pilot understand what a manned pilot is about to do at 300–1,000 ft.
- **Predictable limits.** 400 ft, 100 mph, 3 SM, 500 / 2,000 cloud clearance, and VLOS exist so other people can roughly predict where small UA will be.
- **Proactive risk control.** IMSAFE, PAVE, and DECIDE exist because most drone mishaps are decision failures, not mysterious physics.
- **Scalable commerce.** Categories 1–4, waivers, LAANC, and Remote ID are how the FAA tries to allow economic use without giving every operator unlimited access to people and controlled airspace.

If you study only memorized numbers, you can still fail chart and ADM questions. If you study only how your drone flies, you will miss the regulatory items that are graded in black and white.

## Exam-day tactics

1. Read every figure number. Open the supplement before answering.
2. For airspace questions, use the floor printed on the chart, not a memorized “typical” dimension.
3. Visibility is from the **control station**, not from the drone.
4. Authorization is not a waiver.
5. Night is legal with current training and a 3 SM strobe.
6. Eliminate answers that ignore yield-to-manned or VLOS.
7. If two answers look right, take the more conservative safety / regulation answer.
8. Flag hard chart questions and return. Do not burn eight minutes on one MEF.

Bring government photo ID and your FTN. Sleep and eat. IMSAFE applies to test day too.
