# Part 107 Exam Cram

60 questions · 2 hours · 70% to pass (42/60) · ~$175 at PSI · retake wait 14 days  
Bring government ID + FTN. You get the testing-supplement figures booklet. Use it.

## Memorize these numbers first

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Max groundspeed | **87 kt / 100 mph** |
| Max altitude | **400 ft AGL** |
| Structure exception | Within **400 ft** of structure, not more than **400 ft above** the top |
| Min visibility (from control station) | **3 SM** |
| Cloud clearance | **500 ft below**, **2,000 ft horizontally** |
| Anti-collision light (night/twilight) | Visible **3 SM** |
| Accident report deadline | **10 calendar days** |
| Property-damage report trigger | **> $500** repair **or** FMV (not the drone itself) |
| Injury report trigger | **Serious injury** or **loss of consciousness** |
| sUAS weight limit | **< 55 lb** including everything attached |
| Category 1 over people | **≤ 0.55 lb** |
| Registration | **$5 / 3 years**, per aircraft under Part 107 |
| Certificate minimum age | **16** (test at 14) |
| Knowledge recency | Recurrent training every **24 calendar months** |
| Alcohol memory item | **8 hours**, **0.04% BAC**, no impairing drugs |
| Dark adaptation | about **30 minutes** |

Structure trick: you may fly *higher than 400 AGL* only inside a 400-ft radius cylinder around a structure, and never more than 400 ft above that structure.

## Who can fly / papers

- Need a **Remote Pilot Certificate** with sUAS rating, or fly under a remote PIC who can take over immediately.
- English + fit to fly. **No FAA medical**, but you cannot fly with a known unsafe condition.
- Certificate + ID **readily available** when flying.
- Falsifying certificates/records = certificate action.
- **Part 107 registration is required even under 0.55 lb.** Recreational under-0.55 exemption does **not** apply.
- Part 48 (DroneZone) = under 55 lb. Part 47 = 55 lb+ / special cases.
- Mark registration on the outside. Carry proof.

**Remote PIC owns everything:** condition for safe flight, weather, airspace, people on the ground, crew briefing, and the decision to land.

## Operating rules in one page

- **VLOS:** unaided vision (glasses OK). Binoculars/FPV do **not** satisfy VLOS. Must know position, attitude, altitude, direction.
- FPV requires a **visual observer** who keeps unaided VLOS.
- VO + PIC + person at controls must be able to communicate.
- **One drone at a time** for PIC, manipulator, or VO.
- Yield to **all** manned aircraft. Do not create a collision hazard.
- No careless/reckless ops.
- No hazardous materials.
- No dropping objects that hazard people/property.
- No flying from a **moving aircraft**.
- Moving land/water vehicle: only over **sparsely populated** area, and **not** carrying property for hire — unless waived.
- Do not interfere with airport operations or traffic patterns.
- Prohibited/restricted: need the using agency’s permission.
- TFRs/NOTAMs are mandatory. Check every flight.
- External load OK if secure and does not hurt controllability.
- In-state property transport OK if total weight **< 55 lb** and other rules met.
- Emergency: may deviate as needed; report if FAA asks.
- **No transponder / no ADS-B Out** unless authorized.
- Make aircraft available to FAA for inspection.

**Night / twilight**
- Allowed if: training/test **after April 6, 2021** that includes night, **and** anti-collision light visible **3 SM**.
- Night does **not** waive VLOS, weather, airspace auth, or over-people rules.
- If the light dies, land.

## Over people / moving vehicles

Default: **do not fly over people** unless they are:
1. part of the operation, or  
2. under cover / in a **stationary** vehicle that can protect them, or  
3. you meet Category 1–4.

“Over” = over any part of a person, even briefly.

| Cat | Aircraft test | Big operational catch |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ≤ **0.55 lb**, no skin-lacerating exposed rotors | Sustained flight over open-air assemblies needs RID compliance |
| **2** | DoC aircraft; impact ≤ **11 ft-lb**; no lacerating rotors | Labeling, manuals, maintenance; assembly limits |
| **3** | Same injury idea as Cat 2 | Usually **closed/restricted site with notice**, or **no sustained flight** over people; not for open-air assemblies |
| **4** | Part 21 **airworthiness certificate** + Flight Manual allows it | Follow the certificated limitations |

Moving vehicles: must fit a category. Cats 1–3: closed/restricted site with notice, **or** no **sustained** flight over moving vehicles.

Modifying the drone can kill category eligibility.

## Remote ID (Part 89)

- Part 107 flights need **Standard RID** or a **broadcast module**.
- Broadcast from takeoff to shutdown (standard).
- Module ops typically stay in VLOS of the person at the controls.
- RID serial / label matters.
- **ADS-B Out is not RID.**
- Confirm RID works before takeoff.
- Do not bet the exam on “FRIA, so commercial flights skip RID.”

## Waiver vs authorization

| Need | Tool |
|---|---|
| Enter Class **B / C / D / E to the surface** | **Authorization** (LAANC or DroneZone) |
| Break a Part 107 rule (BVLOS, multiple UA, over people outside categories, >100 mph, <3 SM, etc.) | **Waiver** |

LAANC approval does **not** let you bust VLOS, clouds, or over-people rules.

Commonly waivable: moving vehicle, night light provision, VLOS, VO rules, multiple sUAS, right-of-way, over people, certain airspace, 107.51 limits, over moving vehicles.  
VLOS waiver will **not** be issued to carry someone else’s property for hire.

## Airspace cram

Authorization needed in **B, C, D, and Class E surface areas**.  
**Class G:** no class-based ATC auth. Weather minima still apply.

| Class | Chart | Typical shape | sUAS need |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | Solid **blue** | Shelves; numbers like `100/30` = 10,000 ceiling / 3,000 floor **MSL** | Auth |
| C | Solid **magenta** | Core + shelf (often SFC–4,000 AAE, shelf 1,200–4,000) | Auth |
| D | Dashed **blue** | Often SFC–2,500 AAE | Auth |
| E surface | Dashed **magenta** | Around some airports | Auth |
| E 700 | **Magenta** vignette | Floor 700 AGL | No auth *below* that floor |
| E 1,200 | **Blue** vignette | Floor 1,200 AGL | No auth *below* that floor |
| G | Everything under those floors | Uncontrolled | No class auth |

**Special use**
- **P-** Prohibited: stay out
- **R-** Restricted: need controlling agency when hot
- **W-** Warning: offshore military
- **MOA / Alert:** caution, fast military / training
- **CFA:** not charted
- **NSA:** heavy dashed magenta; requested avoidance
- **TFR:** NOTAM, not a standing symbol — still binding
- **MTR IR/VR:** low, fast military
- Mode C veil (~30 NM around B) is a manned transponder idea. You still need **107.41 auth** to enter B/C/D/E-surface.

When tower closes, Class D often reverts to E or G — read the chart/Supplement.

## Sectional symbols that get asked

- **Blue airport** = tower exists (hours in Chart Supplement). **Magenta airport** = no tower.
- Airport pair of numbers: **field elevation / longest runway** (runway in hundreds of feet).
- Star = rotating beacon.
- Obstacle: `1504 (499)` = **1,504 MSL**, **499 AGL**.
- Lighted vs unlighted towers.
- **MEF:** big blue two-digit number in a grid = highest terrain/obstacle (thousands + hundreds MSL) plus buffer. Not your 400-ft clearance card.
- Magenta flag = VFR checkpoint.
- Tick marks / scale bar = measure statute miles.
- Isogonic line = magnetic variation. Runway numbers are **magnetic**.
- Private airport, heliport, seaplane base symbols appear in the supplement legend — look them up on test day instead of guessing.

**Always open the figure.** Floor/ceiling text on the chart beats memory of “typical” dimensions.

## Weather cram

**Go/no-go:** 3 SM vis from the **control station**, plus 500 / 2,000 from clouds.

- Ceiling = lowest **BKN or OVC**
- High + hot + humid + low pressure = **high density altitude** = less lift, worse hover, less endurance
- Unstable air: turbulence, showery precip, towering clouds, good vis between showers
- Stable air: smooth, layer clouds, poor vis, fog/haze
- Cold front: narrow, gusty, CB, then clearing/cooler
- Warm front: stratiform, steady precip, poor vis
- Wind on a METAR is **from** that direction, **true** degrees
- Wind usually stronger with height (surface friction)

**Thunderstorm stages:** cumulus (updrafts) → mature (up + down, rain, lightning, microburst) → dissipating (downdrafts).  
Stay away. Lightning/hail/gust front can exist outside the rain core.

**Fog types:** radiation, advection, upslope, precipitation, steam.

**METAR decoder**  
`KABC 181856Z 18012G20KT 3SM -RA BR BKN008 OVC015 12/11 A2984`
- 18th day, 1856Z
- Wind 180 at 12, gust 20
- 3 SM, light rain, mist
- Broken 800, overcast 1,500 → **ceiling 800 AGL**
- Temp 12 / dew 11 (close spread = fog risk)
- Altimeter 29.84

TAF: `FM` = from, `TEMPO` = temporary, `BECMG` = becoming, `PROB30` = 30% chance.

Sources: METAR, TAF, AIRMET/SIGMET, GFA, ASOS/AWOS, Flight Service / 1800wxbrief, PIREPs.

## Loading / batteries / link

- Weight includes battery + payload + mounts.
- CG off-center = instability, extra motor load, shorter flight.
- Secure the payload. A falling object is a violation if it hazards people/property.
- LiPo: swelling, odor, dents = dead. Charge in a safe container. Store at storage voltage. Cold = sag. Crash = treat as fire risk. Thermal runaway.
- 2.4 GHz vs 5.8 GHz: congestion, obstacles, video vs control.
- Lost link / RTH / GPS loss: know the aircraft will hover, land, or return — PIC still responsible.
- Automation does not move responsibility off the remote PIC.
- Anyone may wrench on a normal 107 drone; **PIC still decides if it is safe**.

## Airports and radio

Monitor CTAF/tower near airports even if you never key the mic.

- Standard pattern = **left** unless charted otherwise.
- Typical pattern altitude ~1,000 AGL airplanes / lower for helicopters — a 400-ft drone can still sit in **final and departure**.
- CTAF = advisory at nontowered fields. UNICOM = FBO/advisory. ATIS = recorded airport info.
- Self-announce: who you are, where you are, intentions.
- Phonetic alphabet will show up.
- Beacon: civil **white-green**; military extra white; heliport **white-green-yellow**.
- Hold-short lines: do not become a ground hazard on movement areas.
- Chart Supplement = tower hours, frequencies, remarks (birds, jumps, part-time tower).
- SIDA = airport security area. Don’t wander airside.

## ADM / human factors (easy points)

**IMSAFE:** Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion/Eating.

**Hazardous attitudes → antidotes**
- Anti-authority → Follow the rules
- Impulsivity → Not so fast. Think first
- Invulnerability → It could happen to me
- Macho → Taking chances is foolish
- Resignation → I can make a difference

**DECIDE:** Detect, Estimate, Choose, Identify, Do, Evaluate  
**PAVE:** Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures

**Night vision**
- Use rods: look **slightly off-center**
- Empty-field myopia and autokinesis
- White light wrecks adaptation
- Unlit towers and wires are the night killer

Meds: if the label says drowsiness, don’t fly.  
Dehydration, heat, fatigue, client pressure = common accident setup.  
CRM: brief the VO, divide the sky, closed-loop comms. PIC still says no.

## Emergencies / reporting

Have a plan for lost link, flyaway, low battery, GPS loss, aircraft toward a runway, Li-ion fire.

Report to FAA within **10 days** if:
- serious injury **or** anyone loses consciousness, or
- other people’s property damage **over $500** (repair or FMV)

Drone smashed in a field, nobody hurt, no other property → **no 107.9 report**.

## Test-day tactics

1. Read every figure number. Open the supplement before answering.
2. For airspace questions, find the **floor printed on the chart**, not the “typical” memory.
3. Visibility is from the **control station**, not from the drone.
4. Authorization ≠ waiver.
5. Night is legal with current training + 3 SM strobe.
6. Eliminate answers that ignore yield-to-manned or VLOS.
7. If two answers look right, pick the more conservative safety/reg answer.
8. Flag chart questions and come back; don’t burn 8 minutes on one MEF.

**Ready bar:** two timed practice tests at **85%+**, and you can recite the number table at the top from memory.

Official backbone if you have one extra hour: ACS `FAA-S-ACS-10B`, current Part 107 on eCFR, and `ALC-451` on FAASafety.gov.
